Research Scientist

For the student who is genuinely comfortable saying, “I don't know yet — let's find out.”

The role
Research scientists ask questions that do not have obvious answers, design experiments or models, analyse evidence and communicate what they discover.

Where it can lead

Research careers exist across physics, chemistry, biology, materials science, environmental science, computing and many interdisciplinary fields. Scientists may work in universities, laboratories, companies, government or research institutes.

Course pathway

Start with a strong undergraduate degree in your chosen science or quantitative field. Research-focused careers commonly involve postgraduate study, with a PhD often expected for independent academic research.

Skills to build

Mathematics · Experimental design · Coding/data analysis · Scientific writing · Critical thinking · Persistence

Three universities known for research

  1. Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT)
  2. California Institute of Technology (Caltech)
  3. University of Cambridge

Start building a research habit

Keep a notebook of questions. Pick one that can be tested with data or a simple experiment, write down your prediction, collect evidence and record what surprised you. Good research begins with a good question.

University examples are starting points, not rankings or guarantees. Research opportunities and admissions requirements vary by field.
