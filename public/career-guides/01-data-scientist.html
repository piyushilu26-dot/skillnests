Data Scientist

A guide for students who like numbers, questions and the satisfaction of finding a pattern nobody noticed.

In one sentence
A data scientist turns messy information into evidence that helps people make better decisions — using statistics, programming and clear thinking.

What the work actually feels like

It is not just “doing AI”. A real data scientist spends time asking the right question, cleaning imperfect data, testing ideas, explaining results and deciding whether a result is actually useful. One day might involve predicting demand; another might involve finding why students are dropping a course.

A sensible study pathway

You can enter through several routes. Common degrees include Computer Science, Data Science, Statistics, Mathematics, Economics or another quantitative field. A bachelor’s degree can be followed by a master’s in Data Science, Statistics, AI or a related area. Your projects matter too: build something, analyse a real dataset and learn to explain what you found.

Who might enjoy it?

If you enjoy mathematics but also want to build things, investigate questions and work with real-world problems, this is worth exploring. You do not need to know every programming language before starting.

Your first small experiment

Pick a dataset about something you genuinely care about — sports, education, climate, prices or music. Ask one question, clean the data, make one useful graph and write five sentences explaining what it means. That is already a tiny version of the job.
