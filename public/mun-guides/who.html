World Health Organization (WHO)

Focus: Global public health · Difficulty: Beginner–Intermediate

Mandate

WHO leads and coordinates international health work. MUN delegates debate health risks and cooperation while considering national health systems, scientific evidence, access and implementation capacity.

How it functions

Delegates represent countries, deliver speeches, caucus, negotiate and draft resolutions or action plans. Strong proposals are evidence-based and explain who will implement them, how they will be financed and how results will be measured.

Typical agendas

  • Pandemic preparedness and response
  • Vaccination and medicine access
  • Antimicrobial resistance
  • Maternal and child health
  • Mental health
  • Health-system resilience

Research

Study national health indicators, health expenditure, relevant WHO frameworks, previous outbreaks and your country's position on access to medicines and international cooperation.

Bloc strategy

Build coalitions around shared health outcomes. Combine prevention, surveillance, research, workforce, financing and equitable access instead of relying on a single intervention.

Delegate checklist

  • Know your country's health strengths and gaps.
  • Use reliable public-health evidence.
  • Prepare practical funding mechanisms.
  • Address low-resource settings where relevant.
  • Avoid unsupported medical claims.
