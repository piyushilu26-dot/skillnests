UN General Assembly (UNGA)

Type: General Assembly committee · Difficulty: Beginner–Intermediate

What is UNGA?

The General Assembly is the UN's main deliberative and representative body. In MUN, delegates represent member states and debate broad international questions. Most UNGA resolutions are recommendations, so delegates should focus on realistic international cooperation rather than treating them as domestic law.

How it functions

Delegates give opening speeches, participate in moderated and unmoderated caucuses, form blocs, negotiate working papers and submit draft resolutions. The chair manages the speakers list, motions and voting. Delegates must remain consistent with their country's foreign policy.

Typical agenda areas

  • International peace and security
  • Disarmament and non-proliferation
  • Sustainable development and poverty
  • Migration and refugees
  • Technology, AI and global governance
  • Humanitarian emergencies

What to research

  • Your country's official position and allies
  • Relevant UN resolutions, treaties and programmes
  • Previous international initiatives
  • Economic, legal and political feasibility of proposed solutions

Bloc strategy

Build a coalition around shared interests, not just geography. Agree on the problem, then divide solutions into prevention, implementation, financing and monitoring. Strong delegates compromise on wording while protecting their country's core interests.

Resolution basics

A strong draft resolution identifies the problem, proposes specific actions, assigns responsible actors, considers funding and includes measurable implementation or review mechanisms.

Delegate checklist

  • Prepare a one-page country policy brief.
  • Prepare a 60–90 second opening speech.
  • Know at least five allies and likely opponents.
  • Prepare evidence for your strongest claims.
  • Practice concise POIs, speeches and negotiation.

Common mistake

Do not propose actions outside the committee's mandate or assume every UN resolution is legally binding. Feasibility and diplomatic language matter.
