Indian Parliament — Lok Sabha

Role

The Lok Sabha is the directly elected House of India's Parliament. In a parliamentary simulation, delegates usually represent MPs or parties and debate legislation and national policy.

How it functions

The conference may simulate questions, motions, speeches, bills, amendments and voting. Follow the specific committee's rules because MUN versions can differ from real parliamentary procedure.

Agenda areas

  • Economic and fiscal policy
  • Education and health
  • Internal security
  • Agriculture and employment
  • Technology and governance

Research

Study the assigned party or MP's ideology, manifesto, voting record where relevant, constituency interests and current policy position. Know the constitutional division of powers.

Strategy

Build party alliances, negotiate amendments and defend positions consistently. Strong speeches combine evidence, policy details and parliamentary relevance.

Checklist

Prepare party briefs, likely allies, counterarguments, statistics and two or three concrete amendments.
