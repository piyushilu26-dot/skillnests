UN Security Council (UNSC)

Type: Security committee · Difficulty: Intermediate–Advanced

Mandate

The UNSC has primary responsibility for international peace and security. In MUN, delegates address conflicts, threats to peace, sanctions, peacekeeping, arms issues and crisis situations.

How it functions

Delegates represent states, including the five permanent members in simulations that include them. Debate usually moves quickly through speeches, moderated caucuses, informal negotiation and draft resolutions. Voting is central, and veto rules may be simulated depending on conference procedure.

Agenda areas

  • Armed conflict and ceasefires
  • Peacekeeping operations
  • Sanctions and enforcement
  • Weapons proliferation
  • Territorial disputes
  • Protection of civilians

Research

Study your country's alliances, military and diplomatic interests, relevant treaties, previous UNSC resolutions and the positions of the other major powers. Know the legal basis of proposed actions.

Bloc strategy

Security Council blocs are fluid. Identify red lines early, then negotiate language that can attract enough votes. Permanent members should understand veto leverage; elected members should use coalition-building and agenda-setting.

Strong solutions

Consider ceasefire mechanisms, monitoring missions, humanitarian access, sanctions design, peacekeeping mandates, reporting requirements and diplomatic processes. Avoid vague calls for "more cooperation" without implementation details.

Delegate checklist

  • Know your country's strategic interests.
  • Prepare a short opening speech.
  • Map P5 and regional positions.
  • Know which actions are realistic for the Council.
  • Practice fast negotiation and crisis responses.

Common mistake

Do not treat the UNSC like a general policy forum. Security, mandate, voting power and geopolitical feasibility should shape every major proposal.
